# Constructive Analysis Plan

This plan extends the existing HoTT-style Cauchy-real development into a
small Bishop-style analysis library.  The guiding choice is to work with
explicit rational precision data: moduli of continuity, moduli of convergence,
finite rational nets, and approximate conclusions where exact classical
existence is not constructively valid.

## Literature Basis

- The HoTT Book, Chapter 11, defines Cauchy reals by a higher
  inductive-inductive construction with rational-indexed closeness and limits:
  https://homotopytypetheory.org/book/
- Gilbert, *Formalising Real Numbers in Homotopy Type Theory* (2016), treats
  the HoTT Cauchy reals as the free Cauchy-complete metric space generated by
  the rationals and emphasizes Lipschitz maps as the stable extension
  interface: https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05072
- Booij, *The HoTT reals coincide with the Escardo-Simpson reals* (2017),
  identifies HoTT reals with the least Cauchy-complete subset of Dedekind
  reals containing the rationals and connects them to an interval universal
  property: https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.05956
- Brough, *Formalizing the Real Numbers in Homotopy Type Theory with Cubical
  Agda* (2026), confirms that the HoTT Book construction can be formalized
  directly in Cubical Agda without postulates or quotient-sequence
  bookkeeping: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24782
- Bishop, *Foundations of Constructive Analysis* (1967), and
  Bishop-Bridges, *Constructive Analysis* (1985), are the main source for the
  intended theorem style: modulated sequences, constructive order, compactness
  through total boundedness plus completeness, and approximate analysis
  principles.
- Murray, *Constructive Analysis in the Agda Proof Assistant* (2022), shows a
  practical Agda route through Bishop real numbers, arithmetic, ordering,
  uncountability, and Cauchy completeness:
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.08354
- Frank, *Interpolating Between Choices for the Approximate Intermediate Value
  Theorem* (2020), supports making IVT approximate rather than exact:
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.02227
- Petrakis, *McShane-Whitney extensions in constructive analysis* (2018),
  supports focusing first on Lipschitz and modulus-controlled uniformly
  continuous functions on totally bounded spaces:
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06757

## Scope

The new library should live under `Constructive/Analysis/`.  The
`Constructive/CauchyReals/` namespace should remain the public construction
and algebra/order interface for `ℝᶜ`; analysis-specific notions should import
that interface rather than being added back into the real-number construction.

The first target is not classical undergraduate real analysis verbatim.  It is
the Bishop-compatible core:

- metric and uniform structure with explicit positive-rational precision;
- sequences with explicit convergence and Cauchy moduli;
- uniformly continuous and Lipschitz maps;
- total boundedness by finite nets;
- compactness as total boundedness plus Cauchy completeness;
- approximate interval theorems;
- fixed-point and series results whose proofs are constructive with explicit
  bounds.

## Non-Goals

- Do not prove exact IVT for arbitrary continuous functions.
- Do not assert that pointwise continuity on `[a,b]` gives uniform continuity
  without an explicit modulus, compactness principle, or separately stated
  assumption.
- Do not define arbitrary suprema or Bolzano-Weierstrass for arbitrary bounded
  sequences constructively.  Use located/approximated variants or keep such
  theorems in the classical namespace.
- Do not introduce a second real-number construction for Bishop reals unless a
  comparison theorem is the explicit target.

## Phase 1: Metric And Uniform Core

Create:

- `Constructive/Analysis/Metric.agda`
- `Constructive/Analysis/Metric/Map.agda`
- `Constructive/Analysis/Metric/Complete.agda`
- `Constructive/Analysis/Metric/TotallyBounded.agda`

Define a precision-indexed metric interface compatible with current Cauchy
closeness:

- a carrier `A`;
- a relation `Close : A -> ℚ⁺ -> A -> Type`;
- reflexivity, symmetry, triangle, roundedness when needed;
- separatedness as `(∀ ε -> Close x ε y) -> x ≡ y`.

Then define:

- nonexpanding maps;
- Lipschitz maps by precision moduli;
- uniformly continuous maps by explicit precision moduli;
- composition, identity, constants;
- Cauchy approximations indexed by `ℚ⁺`;
- completeness as existence of limits for such approximations.

Reuse `Constructive.CauchyReals.Completeness` where possible, but avoid thin
aliases.  Promote genuinely generic definitions only if they shorten later
analysis modules.

Expected public theorem:

- `CauchyRealsMetricSpace`
- `CauchyRealsIsComplete`
- uniformly continuous maps preserve Cauchy approximations;
- uniformly continuous maps into complete spaces extend over Cauchy limits
  when the source data is dense enough.

## Phase 2: Real Sequences

Create:

- `Constructive/Analysis/Reals/Sequences.agda`
- `Constructive/Analysis/Reals/Sequences/Algebra.agda`
- `Constructive/Analysis/Reals/Sequences/Order.agda`

Use `ℕ -> ℝᶜ` for ordinary sequences, but require moduli for constructive
theorems:

- `ConvergesWithModulus u x μ`;
- `CauchyWithModulus u μ`;
- uniqueness of limits;
- constant sequences;
- subsequences with monotone index maps;
- algebra of limits for addition, negation, multiplication, inverse under
  apartness from zero;
- order lemmas for limits with apartness or eventual bounds.

Expected public theorem set:

- convergence implies Cauchy;
- Cauchy with modulus has a limit in `ℝᶜ`;
- sums/products of convergent sequences converge;
- squeeze theorem in an approximate/order-apart form;
- uniqueness of limits by `Constructive.CauchyReals.path`.

## Phase 3: Series And Elementary Bounds

Create:

- `Constructive/Analysis/Reals/Series.agda`
- `Constructive/Analysis/Reals/Series/Instances/Geometric.agda`

Define partial sums and convergence with explicit tails:

- finite sums over `Fin n`;
- geometric series for rational and real ratios with explicit `|r| < 1`;
- comparison test with a modulated majorant;
- Cauchy criterion for series with explicit tail bounds.

Expected public theorem set:

- convergence of absolutely summable series with a tail modulus;
- geometric-series convergence;
- Neumann-series style inverse for `1 - x` when `|x| < 1`, if the order and
  reciprocal API make the bounds manageable.

This phase supports later elementary functions and Banach fixed-point proofs.

## Phase 4: Intervals And Total Boundedness

Create:

- `Constructive/Analysis/Real/Interval.agda`
- `Constructive/Analysis/Real/Interval/Grid.agda`
- `Constructive/Analysis/Real/Interval/Compact.agda`

Define closed intervals as subtypes:

- `[ a , b ]ᶜ = Σ[ x ∈ ℝᶜ ] (a ≤ᶜ x × x ≤ᶜ b)`;
- maps from rational grids into intervals;
- finite `ε`-nets for intervals;
- interval total boundedness.

Expected public theorem set:

- endpoints inhabit `[a,b]` when `a ≤ᶜ b`;
- interval inclusion and monotonicity;
- finite rational-grid cover for `[a,b]`;
- uniformly continuous image of a totally bounded space is totally bounded;
- approximate maximum and minimum for uniformly continuous
  `f : [a,b]ᶜ -> ℝᶜ`.

Exact maxima should be delayed until the library has the compactness notion
and locatedness assumptions needed to state them constructively.

## Phase 5: Approximate Intermediate Value Theorem

Create:

- `Constructive/Analysis/Real/IVT/Approximate.agda`

Target theorem:

```agda
approximate-IVT :
  (a b : ℝᶜ) ->
  a ≤ᶜ b ->
  (f : [ a , b ]ᶜ -> ℝᶜ) ->
  UniformlyContinuous f ->
  f leftEndpoint <ᶜ 0ᶜ ->
  0ᶜ <ᶜ f rightEndpoint ->
  (ε : ℚ⁺) ->
  Σ[ x ∈ [ a , b ]ᶜ ] absᶜ (f x) <ᶜ rational (radius ε)
```

The exact shape may change to match the local order API; the essential point
is the approximate conclusion `|f x| < ε`.

Implementation route:

- prove a finite-grid sign-change or near-zero lemma;
- use uniform continuity to transfer grid precision to function precision;
- avoid binary choices that require deciding `f m ≤ 0` or `0 ≤ f m` unless the
  comparison is approximate and rationally separated.

This follows Frank's formulation and should remain separate from the
classical exact IVT already present under `Classical/Analysis`.

## Phase 6: Banach Fixed Point

Create:

- `Constructive/Analysis/FixedPoint.agda`
- `Constructive/Analysis/FixedPoint/Banach.agda`

Target theorem:

- for a complete metric space and a contraction with explicit rational
  constant `c < 1`, Picard iterates form a Cauchy approximation with an
  explicit modulus;
- the limit is a fixed point;
- the fixed point is unique.

For `ℝᶜ`, support concrete corollaries for closed intervals once interval
invariance and completeness are available.

Although the theorem is stated for metric spaces, fixed-point principles should
live under `Constructive.Analysis.FixedPoint` rather than under
`Constructive.Analysis.Metric`.  The metric namespace should remain the home of
metric structures, maps, Cauchy approximations, and total-boundedness
interfaces; fixed-point theorems should import those interfaces.

This phase is high value because it exercises completeness, sequences,
geometric-series bounds, and uniformly continuous maps without requiring
nonconstructive compactness.

## Phase 7: Lipschitz Approximation And Extension

Create:

- `Constructive/Analysis/Metric/LipschitzExtension.agda`
- `Constructive/Analysis/Real/UniformApproximation.agda`

Target theorem set:

- McShane-style extension for suitable real-valued Lipschitz maps;
- approximate extension variants where exact infimum is not constructively
  available;
- Lipschitz functions are uniformly dense in uniformly continuous functions on
  totally bounded spaces, in the modulus-controlled form used by Petrakis.

This should reuse the existing `Constructive.CauchyReals.Extension` and
`Constructive.CauchyReals.Lipschitz` ideas, but the general metric-space
statements belong under `Constructive/Analysis/Metric`.

## Phase 8: Elementary Functions

Create:

- `Constructive/Analysis/Real/PowerSeries.agda`
- `Constructive/Analysis/Real/Elementary.agda`

Start with functions whose constructive convergence proof is direct:

- powers and polynomial maps;
- exponential by power series with explicit tail bounds;
- sine and cosine after factorial and alternating-series infrastructure;
- square root either by interval bisection with approximate roots or by a
  contraction/Newton method on a bounded interval.

Each function should come with:

- domain statement;
- convergence modulus;
- continuity or uniform continuity on bounded intervals;
- basic algebraic identities only when the required series rearrangements have
  explicit bounds.

## Phase 9: Integration And Documentation

Add aggregate modules only after the underlying modules stabilize:

- `Constructive/Analysis.agda`
- `Constructive/Analysis/Real.agda`

Update `README.md` when the new namespace becomes public.  The README should
state exactly which results are constructive and approximate.  Classical
counterparts should remain documented under `Classical/Analysis`.

## Suggested Implementation Order

1. Build the generic metric interface only as far as needed by `ℝᶜ`.
2. Prove the real-sequence API and Cauchy criterion.
3. Prove interval total boundedness by rational grids.
4. Prove uniformly continuous images preserve total boundedness.
5. Prove approximate extrema.
6. Prove approximate IVT.
7. Prove Banach fixed point.
8. Add series and elementary functions when the bound library is mature enough.

This order keeps each phase testable and avoids importing classical compactness
or exact choice principles into the constructive namespace.
